# Offer and pricing

## The ladder

### 1. Transferability Scorecard — free

Twenty questions, answered in about six minutes, no account. Returns a transferability grade,
a rough value range, and the single biggest gap.

**Why free, and why private.** The owner's first fear is not cost, it is judgement: being
told that thirty years of work is worth less than they hoped, by a stranger, possibly in
front of their accountant. The scorecard lets them find out alone. That is the reason it
exists — the lead generation is a by-product.

**It is deliberately incomplete.** It gives a grade, not a priced work order. The full
appraisal needs real financials, and that is the paid product.

### 2. Readiness Report — $1,200 fixed

The appraisal engine run on their actual numbers, delivered in a 90-minute session.

- Normalised SDE with every add-back itemised as a buyer's accountant would read it
- What the business would fetch today, as a range, and the multiple behind it
- **What each gap is worth in dollars**, ranked by value per month of effort
- What it would be worth with all of them closed
- A verdict — including, where warranted, "not listable as it stands"

**Why $1,200.** Below the threshold where a small-business owner needs to think about it for
a week, and high enough to filter out the merely curious. An owner who will not pay $1,200 to
learn what their life's work is worth will not do twelve months of difficult work on it
either — so the price is a qualification mechanism as much as a revenue line.

**Why not free.** A free report is worth what it costs. The paid report is the moment the
owner becomes a client, and the conversion from it is the number the whole business turns on.

### 3. Handover Program — $1,250/month for 12 months

The work itself. A fixed twelve-month engagement with a coach: monthly working sessions,
between-session assignments, and a documented operating manual built as they go.

Roughly in order, because the order is most of the value:

| Months | Work |
| --- | --- |
| 1–3 | Clean the books. Separate personal spending. Get to statements a lender will accept |
| 2–6 | Write the business down — the work, the pricing, the suppliers, the exceptions |
| 4–9 | Move licences, leases and contracts into the company's name |
| 5–12 | Get the owner out of delivery. Name and train a second-in-command |
| 8–12 | Build recurring revenue where the trade allows it |

**Why a fixed term, not a subscription.** Open-ended retainers drift, and an owner signing up
for "until we're done" is signing up for nothing. Twelve months with a defined end is a
commitment they can picture. Clients **graduate** — which is also why the operating model is
built on cohorts rather than churn.

**Why $1,250.** For a business with $300k of SDE that is $15,000 against, in the worked
example, roughly $749,000 of enterprise value — about 2% of the upside. It is also roughly
what the same owner already pays a bookkeeper, so it sits in a familiar budget shape rather
than feeling like a capital decision.

### 4. Transfer File — $3,500

When it is time to list: the diligence package a buyer's lender will ask for, assembled
once — normalised financials with documented add-backs, the operating manual, contract and
licence schedule, customer concentration analysis, staff and compensation summary, and a
re-run appraisal.

**Why it matters.** More than half of deals collapse in due diligence. This is the artefact
that prevents it, and it is worth far more than $3,500 — priced low deliberately, because its
real job is to make the programme's result tangible and to earn the referral to the next
owner.

## What we will not sell

**We do not broker the sale. We do not take a success fee, a commission, or any percentage
of a transaction.** Two reasons, and the second matters more than the first:

1. Brokering a business sale requires a licence in many states. Staying out of it keeps the
   scope clean without a fifty-state legal analysis.
2. A success fee would corrupt the advice. The honest recommendation is sometimes "do not
   sell yet" or "this is not sellable, fix it or wind it down." Nobody paid on completion
   gives that advice reliably.

We refer to brokers and are happy to be referred by them. We are never on both sides.

## Economics

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Blended revenue per customer | ~$5,700 |
| Programme gross margin | ~60% at scale (coach cost is the input) |
| Report delivery cost | ~$90 |
| Partner commission | 20% of partner-sourced revenue |
| Customer acquisition cost | Effectively the commission — no paid advertising in year one |
| Concurrent programmes per coach | 12 |

The last row is the one that governs the business. At twelve concurrent engagements per
coach and $1,250 each, a coach carries about $15,000/month of programme revenue. That number
sets the ceiling, sets the hiring trigger, and is why the model treats capacity as the
constraint rather than demand.

## Price changes we expect to make

- **The Readiness Report is underpriced at $1,200** once there are documented outcomes.
  Expect $1,800–2,500 by month 18. It is being held low on purpose while the
  report-to-programme conversion rate is still being measured — that number matters more
  than the margin on it.
- **The Handover Program will not rise for anyone already in it.** New-client pricing moves
  to $1,500 once a cohort has completed and there are before-and-after appraisals to show.
- **Trade-specific programmes** (HVAC, dental, veterinary, landscaping) will price higher
  than generic ones, because specificity is what this buyer pays for.
