# Go to market — the first 90 days

## The strategic choice

**Distribution is through accountants and bookkeepers.** Everything else follows from this.

The alternative is advertising to 62-year-old owners of plumbing companies, who are not
reading newsletters, not on Hacker News, and correctly suspicious of anyone who approaches
them about their retirement. That path is slow and expensive.

The accountant already has what would take years to earn:

- **Trust**, of the specific kind this decision requires
- **The books** — they can see who is add-back heavy and commingled
- **Timing** — they know who mentioned retiring at the last year-end meeting
- **A reason to care** — losing a client to a business that quietly closes is lost revenue,
  and advisory work is what they are all being told to move into

And critically: **they cannot do this work themselves.** It is not compliance, it takes
twelve months of operational coaching, and it is nothing like what their practice is built
to deliver. So it is a referral, not a competitive threat. Paid 20%, a partner sending four
clients a year earns roughly $13,000 for introductions they are already positioned to make.

## The one number to prove

Everything in the model turns on **report → programme conversion**. Below 20% this is a
consulting practice; the base case assumes 30%; above 30% capacity binds instead of demand.

**It takes about twenty paid reports to measure.** That is the actual goal of the first 90
days. Revenue in this period is a by-product of running the experiment.

## Days 1–30 — ten partners and the first reports

**Goal:** 10 partner agreements, 20 scorecards, 5 paid reports.

1. **Build the partner list.** Independent accounting and bookkeeping practices with 50–400
   small-business clients, in one metro area. Local, not national — the first cohort should
   be close enough to meet in person.
2. **Lead with their client, not with us.** The opening is a free appraisal on one
   anonymised client of their choosing. It demonstrates the product, costs an hour, and
   gives them something to bring to that client themselves.
3. **Make the referral effortless.** A one-page explainer under their letterhead, a
   scorecard link with their code on it, and a standing offer to run the session jointly so
   they are in the room and look good.
4. **Sign ten.** Simple agreement: 20% of what they refer, paid on collection, no exclusivity,
   cancellable by either side.

## Days 31–60 — deliver, and watch the conversion

**Goal:** 15 cumulative reports, first 3 Handover clients, a documented outcome.

1. **Deliver every report personally**, and record every session. The recordings become the
   coach training material — the methodology is the asset, and it is built by doing the work
   before it is written down.
2. **Watch what converts.** Track the report-to-programme rate by trade, by SDE band, by
   whether the owner has a date in mind. The base case assumes 30% overall; the useful
   finding will be which segment converts at 50% and which at 5%.
3. **Publish the first anonymised teardown** — "what a $1.8M HVAC business is actually worth,
   and why" — to partners first, not to the open web. This audience is reached through their
   advisors.
4. **Start the trade pattern.** If HVAC converts, the next ten partners should be the ones
   with HVAC clients. Trade specificity is what this buyer pays for.

## Days 61–90 — decide what this is

**Goal:** 20+ reports delivered, conversion rate known, next two quarters chosen on evidence.

1. **Compute the number.** Twenty reports gives a usable read.
2. **Act on it, honestly:**

   | Conversion | What it means | What to do |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | **Above 30%** | Demand is not the problem | Stop selling. Hire and train the first coach — capacity is now the constraint |
   | **20–30%** | The business works as modelled | Scale partners to 40; keep delivering personally |
   | **10–20%** | Report is valuable, programme is not landing | Restructure the programme — probably shorter and cheaper — before adding partners |
   | **Below 10%** | The thesis is wrong | The report may be a standalone product; the programme is not. Say so and change the plan |

3. **Write the methodology down.** Whatever the number, month three is when the work gets
   documented — partly for coaches, mostly because a business whose method lives in the
   founder's head is exactly the disease this company treats. **Run the product on ourselves
   from the beginning**, and publish our own score. It is the most credible marketing
   available and it costs nothing.

## Channels, ranked

| Channel | Why it works here | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Accountants and bookkeepers** | Trust, books, timing, aligned incentive | 20% commission |
| Trade associations (HVAC, dental, landscaping) | Specificity, and the peer proof this buyer needs | Membership, speaking |
| Business brokers | They want sellable inventory; we are not a rival | Reciprocal referral |
| Community banks and SBA lenders | They want financeable deals | Reciprocal referral |
| SCORE, SBDC, chambers | Free, credible, and exactly this demographic | Time |
| Direct mail | Genuinely still works for this audience | Modest, test after the channel is proven |
| Paid search / social | Wrong audience, wrong moment | Skip in year one |

## What would make us stop

- **Fewer than 10 paid reports from 10 active partners in 90 days.** The channel does not
  work as theorised. Fix the partner motion before anything else.
- **Report-to-programme conversion below 10% across 20+ reports.** The programme is not the
  product. Sell the report and the transfer file, and abandon the coaching model.
- **Reports sell but owners will not act.** If owners buy the diagnosis and refuse the
  treatment, the honest conclusion is that this is an information product, not a services
  business — and the plan should change rather than the goal being restated.
